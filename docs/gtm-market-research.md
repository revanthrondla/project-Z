**GTM Market Research and Launch Plan for an HR, Time Tracking, and
Invoicing Application**

**Executive summary**

The strongest initial GTM wedge is **project-based consulting and
professional services**, not all three industries at once. This
recommendation is based on product-fit evidence: consulting firms
already buy software around time capture, utilization, project
profitability, and invoicing, and professional services automation
software is reported as a fast-growing category with consulting firms
representing a meaningful demand segment ([[Fortune Business
Insights]{.underline}](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/professional-services-automation-software-market-109419)).
Agriculture is the best second vertical if the product roadmap supports
offline mobile time capture, crew workflows, piece rates, bilingual
field use, H-2A recordkeeping, and payroll exports, because generic
HR/time tools do not fully match agricultural operations and H-2A labor
usage continues to rise ([[American Farm Bureau
Federation]{.underline}](https://www.fb.org/market-intel/h-2a-program-use-continues-to-soar),
[[U.S. Department of
Labor]{.underline}](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/26-H2A)).
Retail is the weakest immediate wedge for this specific product framing
because retail buyers primarily evaluate scheduling, labor forecasting,
POS integration, shift communication, and payroll workflows, while
invoicing is usually not the core pain point ([[Homebase
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing/), [[Deputy
demand
forecasting]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/features/demand-forecasting)).

The proposed launch strategy is to position the product as an
**operations-to-cash platform for small consulting firms**: capture time
correctly, approve it, turn it into invoices, and understand project
profitability without stitching together spreadsheets, time trackers,
and accounting tools. This is a sharper claim than "HR + time +
invoicing for everyone," and it directly addresses a verified buyer need
in consulting: accurate time tracking, utilization, billing, and
resource visibility are core PSA workflows
([[Deltek]{.underline}](https://www.deltek.com/en/psa/what-is-professional-services-automation),
[[Fortune Business
Insights]{.underline}](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/professional-services-automation-software-market-109419)).

**Scope, assumptions, and evidence rules**

This report assumes the product currently includes or plans to include
employee/contractor records, time tracking, approval workflows, and
invoicing. The research does not assume payroll processing, tax filing,
benefits administration, offline mobile capture, POS integration, AI
scheduling, or H-2A compliance unless explicitly listed as a roadmap
requirement, because those features materially change the GTM plan.

The evidence base uses government labor/compliance sources, vendor
pricing/product pages, market research pages, and review sources. Market
size estimates from commercial research firms should be treated as
directional rather than audited facts, while BLS, DOL, USDA/Farmers.gov,
and [[Employer.gov]{.underline}](http://Employer.gov) claims should be
treated as more authoritative for employment and compliance requirements
([[BLS NAICS 54]{.underline}](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm),
[[BLS Retail
Trade]{.underline}](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag44-45.htm),
[[Employer.gov]{.underline}](https://www.employer.gov/pay-and-benefits/recordkeeping/),
[[Farmers.gov]{.underline}](https://www.farmers.gov/working-with-us/h2a-visa-program)).

**Market landscape**

**Horizontal workforce and time tracking demand**

Workforce management software is reported as a mid-sized, growing
market, with Mordor Intelligence estimating USD 9.36 billion in 2025,
USD 9.76 billion in 2026, and USD 12.04 billion by 2031 at a 4.29% CAGR
([[Mordor
Intelligence]{.underline}](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/workforce-management-software-market-industry)).
The same source reports that time and attendance management held 29.63%
of workforce management revenue in 2025, cloud deployment held 63.81%,
and SMEs are expected to grow adoption faster than large enterprises at
a 6.44% CAGR through 2031 ([[Mordor
Intelligence]{.underline}](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/workforce-management-software-market-industry)).

Time tracking has a stronger SMB fit than enterprise-only workforce
management because one extracted market source reports SMEs at 54%
share, cloud-based deployment at 68%, and subscription pricing at 61%
for the time tracking software market ([[Fortune Business
Insights]{.underline}](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/time-tracking-software-market-111350)).
That supports a SaaS GTM motion for a small-business product, but it
does not by itself prove that one horizontal product can win consulting,
agriculture, and retail simultaneously.

**Consulting and professional services**

Professional services automation appears to be the most directly
adjacent market category for the application because PSA bundles project
management, resource management, time and expense, billing/invoicing,
project accounting, and reporting
([[Deltek]{.underline}](https://www.deltek.com/en/psa/what-is-professional-services-automation)).
Fortune Business Insights reports the PSA software market at USD 14.46
billion in 2025, USD 16.61 billion in 2026, and USD 50.51 billion by
2034, with a 14.91% CAGR, and describes operational efficiency,
predictable project profitability, accurate time tracking, resource
utilization, and billing as key adoption drivers ([[Fortune Business
Insights]{.underline}](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/professional-services-automation-software-market-109419)).

The consulting market is attractive because the buyer pain is close to
revenue. Consulting firms lose money when billable time is missed, time
entries are late, approvals stall, or invoices do not reflect the
contract structure; PSA category materials explicitly connect time and
expense capture to invoicing and project financial management
([[Deltek]{.underline}](https://www.deltek.com/en/psa/what-is-professional-services-automation)).
BLS data also shows that professional, scientific, and technical
services employed about 10.79 million workers in March 2026, which
indicates a large services labor base even though the exact consulting
subsegment must be narrowed during ICP validation ([[BLS NAICS
54]{.underline}](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag54.htm)).

**Agriculture**

Agriculture has a narrower but more differentiated pain point: labor
tracking must handle seasonal work, crews, remote fields, piece rates,
payroll exports, and H-2A compliance.
[[Farmers.gov]{.underline}](http://Farmers.gov) describes H-2A as a
program for temporary or seasonal agricultural work and lists
operational obligations around recruitment, costs, worker guarantees,
and compliance tracking
([[Farmers.gov]{.underline}](https://www.farmers.gov/working-with-us/h2a-visa-program)).
DOL states that H-2A employers must keep accurate records of hours
offered each day and hours actually worked each day, and worker
statements must show hours offered, hours worked, pay rates, piece-rate
units if applicable, total earnings, and deductions ([[U.S. Department
of
Labor]{.underline}](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/26-H2A)).

The agriculture opportunity is validated by specialty competitors, not
by horizontal HR suites. FieldClock positions itself as a farm labor
tracking app that tracks time and production, keeps farms audit-ready,
supports offline operation, and offers month-to-month scalable pricing
for changing seasonal labor forces
([[FieldClock]{.underline}](https://www.fieldclock.com)). Agri-Trak
positions itself as an all-in-one labor tracking and H-2A compliance
solution with farm dashboards, hourly and piece-rate tracking, payroll
reports, three-fourths guarantee verification, and offline field tools
([[Agri-Trak]{.underline}](https://www.agri-trak.com)). Time Portal
positions itself as an agricultural time clock and H-2A recordkeeping
tool with hours-offered tracking, piece work tracking, crop/location/job
tracking, minimum wage calculations, and payroll export files ([[Time
Portal]{.underline}](https://www.timeportal.io)).

H-2A growth strengthens the case for an agriculture vertical, but it
also raises product requirements. The American Farm Bureau Federation
reported 398,258 H-2A positions certified in FY 2025, growth of 13,358
certified positions from FY 2024, 185% growth over ten years, and only
182 domestic applicants for more than 415,000 advertised positions
([[American Farm Bureau
Federation]{.underline}](https://www.fb.org/market-intel/h-2a-program-use-continues-to-soar)).
A product without H-2A-specific records, three-fourths guarantee
tracking, piece-rate workflows, offline capture, bilingual employee UX,
and payroll integrations should not lead with agriculture until those
capabilities are built.

**Retail**

Retail is a large labor market, but it is not the best initial fit for a
product framed around HR, time tracking, and invoicing. BLS retail trade
data shows about 15.43 million employees in March 2026 and more than
1.06 million private retail establishments in Q3 2025, so the market is
large and fragmented ([[BLS Retail
Trade]{.underline}](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag44-45.htm)). However,
retail workforce software competitors emphasize scheduling, time clocks,
payroll, team communication, POS data, labor forecasting, and
location-level labor control rather than invoicing ([[Homebase
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing/), [[Deputy
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/pricing), [[Deputy demand
forecasting]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/features/demand-forecasting)).

Homebase's pricing page shows a location-based model with a free Basic
tier for one location up to 10 employees, paid tiers by location,
payroll as an add-on, scheduling, time tracking, team communication, and
higher-tier AI scheduling and labor target features ([[Homebase
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing/)). Deputy's
pricing page shows per-user plans with scheduling, time tracking,
geofencing, biometrics, HR add-ons, payroll add-ons, and a minimum
monthly spend, and its demand forecasting page emphasizes AI labor
optimization using POS and payroll data ([[Deputy
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/pricing), [[Deputy demand
forecasting]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/features/demand-forecasting)).
Those patterns imply that retail buyers will ask for features that are
not central to an invoicing-led product.

**Recommended GTM strategy**

**Beachhead ICP**

The initial ICP should be **5-75 person consulting and professional
services firms** with mixed employees and contractors, project-based
work, hourly or retainer billing, and a finance/admin owner who
currently reconciles time, invoices, and HR records across spreadsheets,
QuickBooks, Gusto, Harvest, or manual workflows. This ICP is specific
enough to target but broad enough to include IT consulting, fractional
finance, marketing agencies, implementation partners, and small
management consulting firms.

The buyer is likely the founder, operations manager, finance lead, or
practice manager, not a traditional HR leader. The strongest economic
buyer pain is revenue leakage and delayed cash collection, because PSA
category evidence connects time and expense capture directly to billing
accuracy, project profitability, and cash flow
([[Deltek]{.underline}](https://www.deltek.com/en/psa/what-is-professional-services-automation)).

**Positioning**

For small consulting firms, the product should be positioned as the
**time-to-cash operating system** that turns approved work into accurate
invoices and gives owners visibility into project margins without
implementing a heavy PSA. The positioning should avoid claiming to be a
full HRIS, payroll provider, or enterprise PSA until those feature sets
are proven.

Recommended positioning statement:

> For small consulting and professional services firms, the product is a
> lightweight time-to-invoice operations platform that helps owners
> capture billable work, approve time, manage contractor and employee
> records, and send accurate invoices because it connects time, people,
> projects, and billing in one workflow.

user counts only. Competitors condition the market to expect seat-based
or base-plus-seat pricing, as seen in Harvest, QuickBooks Time, Gusto,
and Deputy pricing pages
([[Harvest]{.underline}](https://www.getharvest.com/pricing),
[[QuickBooks
Time]{.underline}](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/time-tracking/pricing/),
[[Gusto]{.underline}](https://gusto.com/product/pricing),
[[Deputy]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/pricing)).

**Acquisition channels**

The first channel should be founder-led outbound to consulting operators
because the problem is workflow-specific and benefits from direct
discovery. Target search phrases and landing pages should focus on
"consulting time tracking invoicing," "billable hours invoice approval,"
"Harvest alternative for consulting firms," "QuickBooks Time alternative
for consultants," and "time tracking for contractors and employees."

The second channel should be integration marketplaces and partner
referrals. QuickBooks Time's positioning relies heavily on accounting
and payroll sync, Harvest highlights QuickBooks, Xero, and Stripe
invoice/payment sync, and Time Portal emphasizes payroll import files
for agricultural payroll software, which shows that buyers expect time
tools to fit into accounting and payroll workflows rather than replace
everything immediately ([[QuickBooks
Time]{.underline}](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/time-tracking/pricing/),
[[Harvest]{.underline}](https://www.getharvest.com/pricing), [[Time
Portal]{.underline}](https://www.timeportal.io)).

The third channel should be content-led demand capture. Publish
calculators and templates around billable utilization, invoice leakage,
time approval SLAs, project margin by client, and contractor vs employee
workflows. This matches the consulting buyer's financial pain and avoids
generic HR content that would compete against high-authority HRIS
vendors.

**Sales motion**

Use a low-touch sales-assisted motion for the first 50 customers. A pure
self-serve funnel will likely miss the workflow detail needed to shape
product-market fit, while a heavy enterprise motion is too expensive for
SMB consulting firms.

The core discovery questions should be:

-   How many billable people submit time weekly?

-   How many invoice formats or billing models are active: hourly,
    retainer, fixed fee, milestone, or mixed?

-   What percentage of time entries arrive late or require admin
    follow-up?

-   How many tools are touched between time entry and invoice send?

-   How does the firm track project profitability today?

-   Are contractors and employees handled differently?

-   Which accounting/payroll systems must remain source of truth?

**Agriculture expansion strategy**

Agriculture should be a separate vertical product motion, not a generic
landing page. The minimum viable agriculture package must include
offline mobile capture, crew clock-in/out, crop/location/job tracking,
hourly and piece-rate support, bilingual UX, payroll exports, audit
reports, and H-2A-specific fields for hours offered, hours worked,
piece-rate units, three-fourths guarantee status, deductions, and
earnings statements because DOL requires those record types for H-2A
employers ([[U.S. Department of
Labor]{.underline}](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/26-H2A)).

If those features are built, target specialty crop growers, farm labor
contractors, payroll service providers serving farms, and growers using
H-2A labor in high-usage states. FieldClock, Agri-Trak, and Time Portal
prove that farmers will evaluate vertical credibility, offline
reliability, crew workflows, and compliance value, not generic HR
terminology ([[FieldClock]{.underline}](https://www.fieldclock.com),
[[Agri-Trak]{.underline}](https://www.agri-trak.com), [[Time
Portal]{.underline}](https://www.timeportal.io)).

**Retail deferral strategy**

Retail should be deferred until the product includes scheduling, shift
swaps, availability, time clocks, geofencing, POS data, payroll handoff,
and labor forecasting. Retail competitors already condition buyers
around these capabilities, and Deputy explicitly positions AI labor
optimization around POS and payroll data while Homebase bundles
scheduling, time tracking, payroll, HR, and communication by location
([[Deputy demand
forecasting]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/features/demand-forecasting),
[[Homebase
pricing]{.underline}](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing/)).

If retail is later pursued, focus on specialty retail businesses with
1-5 locations that have simple scheduling needs and do B2B services or
custom orders where invoicing matters. Avoid multi-location chains at
the start because they will compare against Homebase, Deputy, When I
Work, 7shifts, POS-native scheduling, and payroll providers.

**Product requirements implied by GTM**

**Consulting MVP**

-   **Time capture:** Timers, weekly timesheets, manual time, reminders,
    billable/non-billable classification, notes required by project.

-   **Approvals:** Manager/client approval, change history, rejection
    reasons, locked periods, audit log.

-   **Projects and billing:** Client, project, task, rate card,
    retainer/fixed-fee/hourly billing, invoice drafts, invoice status.

-   **Profitability:** Estimated versus actual hours, realization,
    utilization, project gross margin, write-offs, unbilled time aging.

-   **People operations:** Employee/contractor records, role
    permissions, onboarding checklist, document storage, basic
    PTO/time-off visibility.

-   **Integrations:** QuickBooks Online, Xero, Stripe, Gusto export, CSV
    import/export, calendar import, Slack/email reminders.

**Agriculture MVP, only if agriculture is chosen**

-   **Field time:** Offline mobile, kiosk/tablet mode, crew
    clock-in/out, supervisor entry, geofencing optional but not required
    where signal is poor.

-   **Farm dimensions:** Crop, block, row, location, job, crew,
    piece-rate units, hourly work, equipment if relevant.

-   **Compliance:** H-2A hours offered, hours worked, three-fourths
    guarantee tracking, piece-rate unit statements, deductions, earnings
    statements, audit reports.

-   **Language and usability:** English/Spanish UX, low-literacy
    workflows, minimal taps, supervisor-first operations.

-   **Payroll:** Export files for farm payroll providers, configurable
    pay rules, minimum wage make-up calculations, exception reports.

**Retail MVP, only if retail is chosen**

-   **Scheduling:** Availability, templates, shift swaps, open shifts,
    break rules, notifications.

-   **Time clock:** Mobile/tablet/POS time clock, photos or PIN,
    geofencing, timecard approvals.

-   **Operations:** Team chat, tasks, location and department
    permissions, payroll export.

-   **Optimization:** Labor budget and, later, POS-linked demand
    forecasting.

**0-90 day execution plan**

**Days 0-15: Narrow ICP and validate pain**

Interview 20-30 consulting firms with 5-75 billable workers. The goal is
not to pitch; it is to measure how often firms experience late time
entry, invoice delays, write-offs, tool fragmentation, and project
margin blind spots.

Deliverables:

-   ICP scorecard by firm size, billing model, and current tool stack.

-   Problem frequency and willingness-to-pay notes.

-   Top 10 integration requests.

-   Current workflow map from time entry to invoice.

**Days 16-30: Build the consulting wedge message**

Launch one focused website page and two comparison pages: "Harvest
alternative for consulting firms" and "QuickBooks Time alternative for
consulting firms." The pages should not attack competitors; they should
reframe around workflow outcomes: fewer late time entries, faster
invoice approvals, less revenue leakage, and clearer project margin.

Deliverables:

-   Consulting landing page.

-   ROI calculator for invoice leakage and admin time.

-   Demo script built around time-to-cash workflow.

-   Waitlist or trial funnel.

**Days 31-60: Run pilot acquisition**

Run founder-led outbound to 300-500 consulting operators and
finance/admin leaders. Offer a concierge migration from spreadsheets,
Harvest, QuickBooks Time, or manual time sheets.

Success criteria:

-   25 discovery calls.

-   10 qualified demos.

-   5 design partners.

-   At least 3 pilots with weekly active time submissions.

**Days 61-90: Convert pilots and harden integrations**

Use pilots to finalize the smallest sellable product. The critical
milestone is not total feature count; it is whether time can move from
consultant to approved invoice with less friction than the current
workflow.

Success criteria:

-   80% weekly time submission rate in active pilot teams.

-   Invoice draft creation from approved time with minimal manual edits.

-   At least two paid conversions or signed LOIs.

-   Clear evidence of one repeated economic buyer pain.

**6-12 month plan**

**Months 4-6: Build repeatability in consulting**

Create a repeatable sales motion around one ICP. Prioritize
integrations, reporting, and onboarding templates over broad HRIS
features. The first scalable growth loop should be migration plus
measurable financial visibility, not generic employee management.

KPIs:

-   Sales-qualified lead to demo conversion.

-   Demo to pilot conversion.

-   Pilot activation within 7 days.

-   Weekly time submission rate.

-   Approved-time-to-invoice cycle time.

-   Gross revenue retention.

-   Expansion from additional users/projects.

**Months 7-9: Decide whether to enter agriculture**

Do not enter agriculture based on TAM alone. Run 15-20 discovery calls
with specialty crop growers, H-2A employers, farm labor contractors, and
farm payroll service providers. The key question is whether the product
can outperform vertical tools on a specific workflow such as H-2A
earnings statements, three-fourths guarantee tracking, or payroll
export.

Go/no-go criteria:

-   At least 5 farms or payroll partners confirm the same urgent
    workflow gap.

-   Offline mobile and crew workflows are feasible.

-   Payroll export requirements are consistent enough to support.

-   A farm-specific compliance advisor or partner can validate claims.

**Months 10-12: Launch agriculture beta or double down on consulting**

If agriculture discovery validates a narrow wedge, launch a separate
beta under farm-specific positioning. If not, double down on consulting
and add deeper PSA-lite features such as utilization planning, retainer
burndown, revenue recognition exports, and client approval portals.

**Final recommendation**

Start with **consulting and professional services** as the beachhead.
The product-market fit logic is strongest because the core product
modules map to revenue workflows, the PSA category validates buyer
demand, and the product can avoid heavily regulated payroll or
agriculture-specific compliance claims at launch
([[Deltek]{.underline}](https://www.deltek.com/en/psa/what-is-professional-services-automation),
[[Fortune Business
Insights]{.underline}](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/professional-services-automation-software-market-109419)).

Treat **agriculture** as a high-differentiation second vertical only if
the roadmap commits to offline farm workflows, H-2A recordkeeping, piece
rates, crew management, bilingual UX, and payroll exports. The H-2A
market signal is real, but the compliance and workflow bar is high
([[U.S. Department of
Labor]{.underline}](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/26-H2A),
[[American Farm Bureau
Federation]{.underline}](https://www.fb.org/market-intel/h-2a-program-use-continues-to-soar)).

Defer **retail** unless the product becomes scheduling-led. Retail is
large, but competitors already anchor buyer expectations around
scheduling, time clocks, location pricing, team communication, payroll,
POS data, and AI labor forecasting, while invoicing is not usually
central to the retail workforce management buying decision ([[BLS Retail
Trade]{.underline}](https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag44-45.htm),
[[Homebase pricing]{.underline}](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing/),
[[Deputy demand
forecasting]{.underline}](https://www.deputy.com/features/demand-forecasting)).
